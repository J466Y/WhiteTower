| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Status** | Version 0.2, with [threat model v0.1.6](threat-model.md) |
| **Date** | 2026-09-30; updated 2026-10-19 (RFC-0002: MCP servers, tools and models) and 2026-10-27 (ISO/IEC 42001, the GDPR, and the evidence of each article and control: the maintainer's decision) |
| **Plan** | [P0-04](../plans/phase-0/P0-04-threat-model.md), step 7; requirement NFR-22 |

How White Tower's MVP relates to four frameworks its users will be asked about: the OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026, the EU AI Act, ISO/IEC 42001 and the GDPR. Every claim names the requirement that delivers it, so it can be checked against the implementation. Every article and control also names its evidence: the types of the audit log's events that show it, which an evidence bundle hands to an auditor (section 5). White Tower is a governance tool: it supports these frameworks' goals for the agents it governs, and does not by itself make an agent or an organization compliant.

Event types are named without their prefix and version: `decision.made` is `whitetower.decision.made.v1` ([event catalog](../../api/events/catalog.json)). `catalog.*` and `tooling.*` are the events of the catalog and of agents' tooling that plan P1-16 adds (RFC-0002, section 9).

## 1. OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026

The list published by the [OWASP GenAI Security Project](https://genai.owasp.org/2025/12/09/owasp-top-10-for-agentic-applications-the-benchmark-for-agentic-security-in-the-age-of-autonomous-ai/) in December 2025. For each risk: what the MVP provides, what later phases add, and what stays outside White Tower.

| ID | Risk | In the MVP | Later | Outside White Tower |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

## 2. EU AI Act

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 ([ELI](http://data.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj)), as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, the Digital Omnibus on AI ([ELI](http://data.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj)).

**How it applies.** Whether an agent is a high-risk AI system depends on its intended purpose (Article 6 and Annex III), so the obligations below apply to some governed agents and not to others. Each use case records the classification that people give it under the Act (`prohibited`, `high_risk`, `limited`, `minimal` or `not_assessed`), which shows which agents the obligations concern; White Tower does not classify. White Tower itself makes no decisions about people and is not a high-risk system. It gives the providers and deployers of agents capabilities that support their obligations; conformity depends on the whole system and its documentation.

| Article | What it requires | Who | What White Tower provides | Evidence | Requirements |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | Some AI practices are prohibited | Providers and deployers | A use case classified as prohibited can never be validated, so its agent can never be activated ([lifecycle](../architecture/lifecycle.md), guard `use_case_not_prohibited`) | `use_case.created`, `use_case.updated`, `agent.lifecycle_changed` | INV-03, INV-04 |
| 12(1) and (2) | High-risk systems allow the automatic recording of events (logs) over their lifetime, so that risk situations can be identified and the system's operation monitored | Providers, by design | The enforcement point records every decision and governed action automatically, and the core every governance change, in a tamper-evident log with trace context, exported to the organization's systems | `decision.made`, `action.executed`, every event of the core, and the `audit.checkpoint` events that seal them | AUD-01 to AUD-06, MOD-06 |
| 14(4)(e) | The people overseeing the system can intervene in its operation or interrupt it "through a 'stop' button or a similar procedure that allows the system to come to a halt in a safe state" | Providers, by design; deployers, in use | The kill switch: per agent, per selector or fleet-wide, from the console, the API or the CLI, without approval. On receipt, the gate closes and every further governed call is denied, which is White Tower's safe state; work in flight is interrupted where the framework allows, and the acknowledgement says what happened at each layer. Break-glass works when the IdP is down; on Kubernetes, the agent is also isolated at the network level | `halt.issued`, `instance.halted`, `quarantine.applied`, `halt.released`, `breakglass.used` | KIL-01 to KIL-10, UI-06, HUM-06 |
| 19(1) | Providers keep the logs their systems generate automatically, to the extent they control them, for at least six months, unless other Union or national law, in particular on personal data, provides otherwise | Providers | Retention of at least six months by default, configurable per event class; dropping older months keeps the rest verifiable | `audit.partition_dropped`, which records each month dropped | NFR-10, AUD-08 |
| 26(6) | Deployers keep the logs generated automatically, to the extent they control them, for at least six months, with the same proviso | Deployers | The same retention, and continuous export to the deployer's own SIEM | `audit.partition_dropped`, and the SIEM's copy of the log | NFR-10, AUD-06, AUD-08 |
| 26(5) | Deployers monitor the system's operation; when its use may present a risk, they inform the provider or distributor and the market surveillance authority, and suspend its use; they report serious incidents | Deployers | Monitoring: the audit explorer, and the decisions, halts, instances and bundles of each agent. Suspending use: lifecycle suspension and halts. The evidence behind a report. Informing the authorities remains the organization's process | `decision.made`, `instance.started`, `bundle.activated`, `bundle.rejected`, `lease.expired`, `halt.issued`, `agent.lifecycle_changed` | INV-04, INV-05, KIL-01, KIL-02, AUD-05, UI-02, UI-05 |
| 26(2) | Deployers assign human oversight to people with the necessary competence, training and authority | Deployers | Every agent has one accountable owner, an active person; committees and roles are explicit, and so is what each role may do | `agent.created`, `agent.ownership_changed`, `principal.deactivated` | INV-02, HUM-02, HUM-03 |
| 26(1) | Deployers use the system in accordance with its instructions for use | Deployers | Approved policies, enforced at runtime, keep each agent within its validated use case | `use_case.created`, `use_case.updated`, `policy.version_approved`, `decision.made` | INV-03, POL-01 to POL-05 |

**Personal data.** The retention articles defer to data protection law. White Tower keeps personal data out of evidence where it can: people appear as pseudonymous IDs, argument values, prompts and outputs are never recorded, and human identifiers can be pseudonymized on export (NFR-20, [contracts section 8.3](../contracts/module-contract-v0.1.md#83-personal-data)). Section 4 maps the GDPR.

**When the high-risk obligations apply.** Articles 12, 14, 19 and 26 are obligations for high-risk systems. The Digital Omnibus on AI deferred them:

| High-risk systems | Apply from | Before the Omnibus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Listed in Annex III (stand-alone uses such as employment, credit or essential services) | 2 December 2027 | 2 August 2026 |
| Products, or safety components of products, under the Union legislation of Annex I | 2 August 2028 | 2 August 2027 |

Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 of 8 July 2026 was published in the Official Journal on 24 July 2026 (OJ L, 2026/1744), and entered into force three days later, on 27 July 2026 (its Article 4). Its Article 1, point (40), rewrites point (c) of Article 113, third paragraph, of the AI Act: Sections 1 to 3 of Chapter III, which hold Articles 12, 14, 19 and 26, apply from the dates in the table above. None of its amendments touches Articles 12, 14, 19 or 26.

High-risk systems placed on the market or put into service before those dates are covered only once their design changes significantly; those intended for public authorities must comply by 2 August 2030 (Article 111(2), as amended).

Checked on 2026-09-30 against the English text and the metadata of the Official Journal, retrieved from the Publications Office's Cellar repository. The dates are kept only in this document, so that a further change is corrected in one place.

White Tower does not carry out the other 28 controls, although it holds facts that some of them use:

- the AI policy (A.2). White Tower's policies are runtime rules for agents, not the AI policy of control A.2.2;
- the reporting of concerns (A.3.3);
- data, computing and human resources (A.4.3, A.4.5 and A.4.6): the inventory records the data categories each agent accesses and the instances that serve it;
- impact assessments (A.5), which start from each agent's risk tier and use case: its purpose, data categories, affected persons and classification under the EU AI Act;
- development and its documentation (A.6.1, A.6.2.2 to A.6.2.4, and A.6.2.7): White Tower governs agents in use, not their development;
- data for AI systems (A.7): White Tower never sees the data agents use;
- information for interested parties (A.8.2, A.8.3 and A.8.5);
- the objectives of responsible use (A.9.3);
- responsibilities shared with third parties, and customers (A.10.2 and A.10.4).

## 4. GDPR

Regulation (EU) 2016/679 ([ELI](http://data.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)).

**Two kinds of processing.** White Tower processes the personal data of the people who govern agents: their names, email addresses and IdP subjects, kept in `principals` and out of evidence ([domain model, section 7](../architecture/domain-model.md#7-personal-data)). It is self-hosted and sends no telemetry, so the organization that runs it is the controller of that processing, and no one else processes the data for it. For the agents it governs, White Tower supports the organization's obligations on the processing those agents do. Whether that processing is lawful (Article 6), and any decision an agent makes about a person (Article 22), remain the organization's to assess.

| Article | What it requires | What White Tower provides | Evidence | Requirements |

**Erasure (Article 17).** Sealed events cannot be edited, so the log names people only by pseudonymous IDs, and keeps them until retention drops their month. The link between an ID and a person lives in `principals`, outside the log. The domain model designs an erasure that replaces a person's name and email there with placeholders and keeps the ID, so that the history stays consistent; no plan builds it yet. Text that people write is copied into events, such as halt reasons and agents' names and labels, so it must not hold personal data.

## 5. Evidence for an audit

An auditor asks for the evidence behind an article or a control. Anyone who may read the audit log can export an evidence bundle (AUD-10, [plan P1-02](../plans/phase-1/P1-02-audit-log.md), step 11), and owners can for their own agents: the events of a time range, filtered by the event types that the tables above name, and by agent if needed. The bundle holds each event exactly as it was sealed, an inclusion proof for each, and the signed checkpoint the proofs lead to.

The auditor checks the bundle offline with `wtctl audit verify --evidence`, with checkpoint keys taken from the organization rather than from the bundle, and finds its checkpoint among those the organization's SIEM holds. A bundle shows that each event it holds is genuine: unaltered, and sealed under a checkpoint that the organization's key signed. It does not show that no matching event was left out: only the whole log shows that, and `wtctl audit verify` checks it.
